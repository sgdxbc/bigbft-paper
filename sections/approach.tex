However, the design combination of state sharding with global processing is rarely explored.
Quote a survey of sharding replication systems from 2023~\cite{solat2023sharding}:
\emph{To the best of our knowledge, actually there is no protocol that uses storage sharding without processing sharding.}

At first glance this may be a costly approach that nodes transferring shards back and forth now and ever.
In fact, it is more practical than it seems like.
As previously mentioned in the problem settings (\autoref{sec:bg}), most transactions are expected to access only single or few shards, so with sufficient number of shards, the amount of transferred data for processing one transaction will be acceptably small.
Also, even in fully replicated systems, there's still a baseline amount of data to be transferred for dispersing the transaction and reply the client, both of each would be $O(number\ of\ node)$ bit complexity of communication.
And last but not least, the real world workload are mostly skewed~\xxx{cite zipf}.
By simply caching a small fraction of shards, the total amount of transferred data can be greatly reduced.

To verify the above claims, we simulate/perform numerical analysis to project the total amount of data transferring under a realistic setup.
