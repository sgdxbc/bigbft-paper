\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Blockchain states are growing big.
The popular permissionless blockchains~\cite{buterin2014next, nakamoto2008bitcoin} are incurring hundreds to thousands of GBs states (\autoref{fig:states-grow}), replicating what into more than ten thousand nodes consumes PBs of storage in total.
The high cost of state replication results in impractically expensive on-chain storage~\cite{balduf2022dude}.
On the other hand, big data applications are vastly deploying on to blockchains~\cite{qi2021bidl,lokhava2019fast,bodkhe2020blockchain,liu2018blockchain,xu2018making,kim2021two,jeong2020design,sun2020blockchain,li2019edurss,shen2019secure,salah2019blockchain,hirtan2018blockchain, sharma2018blockchain}, whose states commonly scale up to TB or even PB level~\cite{laughlin2014information,rabindrajit2024high,pollard2018eicu,johnson2023mimic}.
The large scale deployments of permissioned and consortium blockchains, which consist of hundreds to thousands nodes and often be considered by the previous works~\cite{kokoris2018omniledger,zamani2018rapidchain,amiri2021sharper,gilad2017algorand,gueta2019sbft,yin2019hotstuff}, will be extremely storage-demanding.
To cope with this issue, it is crucial to realize \emph{storage scalability}: scaling up the number of nodes with constant overall storage consumption.

Sharded blockchains, nodes in what can operate while storing only part of the states, are extensively studied by the previous works~\cite{ruan2021blockchains,luu2016secure,kokoris2018omniledger,dang2019towards,zamani2018rapidchain,wang2019monoxide,amiri2021sharper}.
While these works indeed achieve scalable storage demand, the drawback is the trade-offs between storage scalability and the security properties \ie safety and liveness~\cite{pbft}.
As the result, these works cannot retain the standard security guarantee of blockchains \ie Byzantine faulty tolerance (BFT) replication: deterministically tolerate any $f$ Byzantine faulty nodes with a total of at least $3f+1$ nodes.
They may  resort to probabilistic safety guarantee, and propose to tolerate only $\frac{1}{4}$ faulty nodes~\cite{luu2016secure,kokoris2018omniledger} or make various additional requirements like trusted hardware~\cite{dang2019towards} or synchronous network~\cite{zamani2018rapidchain}, while the others demand pre-determined fault-tolerant clusters and can only tolerate $\frac{1}{3}$ Byzantine faults locally within each committee~\cite{ruan2021blockchains,amiri2021sharper}.
In the end, there is no previous sharding solution that can achieve storage scalability without affecting safety and liveness properties.

In this work, we propose a new approach that \emph{decompose} BFT protocol into consensus layer that orders and processes the transactions and storage layer that manages the replicated states and possesses them to consensus layer for transaction processing (\autoref{fig:approach-compare}).
Then, the consensus layer is not sharded and can be scaled up as a whole~\cite{gilad2017algorand,gueta2019sbft,yin2019hotstuff,danezis2022narwhal,spiegelman2022bullshark}, and the storage layer, which is not responsible for consistency, can be simply scaled up with sharding without affecting the security properties.

We emphasize that this \emph{separation of concern} is crucial to escape the dilemma between scalability and security, preventing the coupling of them in the case of processing sharding.

In action, we can pick any off-the-shelf Byzantine atomic broadcast (BAB) or BFT protocol\cite{pbft,gueta2019sbft,yin2019hotstuff,danezis2022narwhal,spiegelman2022bullshark} that features comparable scalability to the storage layer as the consensus layer, and the storage layer should synchronize necessary shards for processing transactions.
Notice that this introduces a new source of liveness violation: if the storage layer fails to ensure the \emph{availability} of (some of) the state shards, nodes cannot process transaction any further even if they are agreed with each other for the processing order.

With this decomposition approach we design \sys, a BFT protocol with scalable storage demand.
The main challenge of designing \sys is to roll out a storage layer that can ensure the availability of state shards in the presence of $\frac{1}{3}$ Byzantine faulty nodes, aligning with the setup of the consensus layer.
Our storage layer is further decomposed into an on-path \emph{active layer} and an off-path \emph{archive layer}.
The active layer maintains constant number of verbatim copies of the shards on randomly selected nodes, ensuring the shards are efficiently accessible with high probability.
The archive layer employs erasure code~\cite{ec}, encoding the shards and dispersing the shards and the parities to a sufficiently wide range of nodes in the background, to provide the fallback deterministic availability guarantee.
Both layers store constant redundancy of the state, so the design achieves the goal of storage scalability.
