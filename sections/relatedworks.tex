\section{Related Works}
\label{sec:relworks}

\paragraph{Data center distributed storage systems deploy erasure codes.}
Ceph~\cite{weil2006ceph}, HDFS~\cite{shvachko2010hadoop}, Azure~\cite{huang2012erasure}.
Morph~\cite{kim2024morph} takes a generational approach, \ie transcoding throughout data lifetime, similar to \sys.
It can to match the ingestion performance of replication while match long term space efficiency of erasure code solutions.
These are general storage layers that do not offer total order to the updates.
They are not design for Byzantine faults either.

\paragraph{Ethereum approach.}
EIP-4844~\xxx{cite} introduces \emph{blobs}, designed to be utilized by rollups for publishing transactions.
Although the blobs are not part of Ethereum's account state, and they have a limited 18-day lifetime, Ethereum guarantees their availability as same as the state (throughout their lifetime).
The blobs are erasure coded to tolerate faults, and the nodes of the placement groups are economically incentivized to persist them.~\xxx{confirm details}
So Ethereum does not provide theoretical availability guarantee and the blobs are read only.

Quote a survey of sharding replication systems from 2023~\cite{solat2023sharding}:
\emph{To the best of our knowledge, actually there is no protocol that uses storage sharding without processing sharding.}

\paragraph{Authenticated data structures.~\cite{martel2004general, tamassia2003authenticated}}
\xxx{Introduce.}
\textsc{Integri}DB~\cite{zhang2015integridb} supports SQL operations.
These works enable interesting alternative approach of \sys by delegating transaction execution to the nodes storing the relevant shards and quorum verifying the to-state, instead of transferring the shards to the executing nodes.
They are yet to support transactions across multiple shards.
