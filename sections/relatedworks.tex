\section{Related Works}
\label{sec:rel}

\paragraph{Availability of historical transactions \ie blocks.}
Alternative to the previous works we discussed in \autoref{sec:bg} that maintain partial states on the nodes, some other works offer availability solutions for the historical transactions.
In such systems storing the processing state explicitly is not necessary.
All committed transactions are made available for a prolonged time and the state (either the current version of any historical one) can be reconstructed by replaying them.

In public blockchains, \emph{archival nodes} volunteer to provision the historical blocks for auditing or analysis purposes.
BFT-store~\cite{qi2020bft} proposes to let the replication nodes collaboratively store the blocks and the design can tolerate $f$ faulty nodes out of the $3f+1$ total ones.
Under permissionless setting, layer-2 \emph{rollups} \xxx{cite} submit their historical transactions to Ethereum's blocks.
EIP-4844~\cite{eip4844} is proposed to extend Ethereum's blocks with \emph{blobs}, which are erasure coded and collaboratively stored by the nodes.
These works have similar designs to \sys, only the blocks instead of the state become the data in interest.
While the historical blocks are an immutable append-only data structure, the processing state is not and poses unique challenges to our work.
